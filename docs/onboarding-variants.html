Groove · design study · onboarding

Five ways into a first beat

Today's editor is a capable instrument panel that assumes you already know what a step sequencer, a piano roll and a sampler are. This study keeps the same three jobs on every variant (make the first beat, choose sounds, arrange it into a song) and changes how much the screen explains itself. Variant 1 only changes the skin. Each one after it changes more of the flow. Every mock plays: press Play, click squares, pick sounds.

What a first-timer meets

  • The project opens on the arrangement, not here. Getting to the steps means knowing the clip must be selected first, then pressing 2, or reading the dock's hover tip.

Choosing a sound today

  • The library is a view, reached by 4 or from a pad's sample slot. It is aimed at whatever was selected, which is invisible unless you read the title row.
  • You must already have decided on an instrument kind (sampler, drum machine, synth) before any sound is offered. The sound you want is downstream of a decision you cannot yet make.
  • The list is good once you are here: audition on Space, insert on Enter, similar sounds a key away.

Arranging today

  • This is the screen a new project opens on. One clip, one bar, no words. The four new-track buttons name engines, not sounds.
  • Every gesture is right by DAW convention (double-click, Alt-drag, loop brace) and invisible without that convention.

Variant 3 · patterns, then a song

Channel rack

FL Studio's model: every sound is a channel, every channel has 16 steps, and a pattern is all of them together. You make patterns first and paint them into a playlist afterwards. The first beat is the first screen, with the kit already loaded.

What changed

  • The default template opens on a rack with Kick, Clap, Hat, Snare and Bass already there. Nothing to add before the first click lands.
  • All channels are on one grid: the hat goes "where the hat row is", not in another track's clip.
  • Pattern / Song is a two-position switch in the transport, which is the whole mental model in one control.
  • Pitched channels (Bass) show their steps too; the piano roll is a detail view opened from the channel name.

What changed

  • Arranging is painting: pick a pattern, click bars. A song of three patterns over eight bars is a dozen clicks, and the beginner never meets a clip edge or an Alt-drag.
  • Patterns can overlap on different lanes (a fill over the main beat), which is how FL makes breakdowns cheap.

What it takes

  • A Pattern in the domain: a named set of clips, one per track, sharing a length. Today a clip belongs to one track and a placement puts one clip at one bar; a pattern is a placement of many clips at once. That is new in src/domain, new commands (pattern.create/paint/clear), new persistence documents and a new audio projection path.
  • A rack view that shows every track's current clip as a row (today's Sequence shows one clip).
  • A channel panel merging Instrument, Library and the track's mixer strip.

Contracts touched

  • Domain schema, command registry, persistence layout, audio and arrangement projections. All of them.
  • Most arrangement flows (CF-009 to CF-016) move behind an advanced timeline or are retired.

Cost

XL
  • A milestone, not a sequence of PRs. The cheapest honest version: a rack view over existing clips (every track's bar-1 clip as a row) with no pattern entity, which gives the first-beat screen but not the song mode.
Variant 4 · pick a sound, not an engine

Sound first

GarageBand's browser crossed with Ableton's Learning Music. You never choose a sampler or a drum machine: you choose Drums, Bass, Keys, Lead or a Loop, from big tiles that play when you hover. Pitched parts write on a grid that only shows notes in the song's key. The song is a row of sections you copy and tweak.

Boldness
Mood Energy
BeatSongSection: Loop · 1 bar

What changed

  • One page called Beat. Drum rows on top, and under them a note grid for each pitched sound showing only the five or seven notes of the song's key, lowest at the bottom. No piano, no black keys, no wrong notes (CF-018's key is set at project creation, with a default).
  • The instrument is gone as a concept. "Mellow Sub" is a sound; what engine plays it is the library's business (a factory asset on a sampler, a preset on a synth).
  • The transport shows the key beside the tempo, because a beginner's second question after "how fast" is "why does that note sound wrong".

What changed

  • The song is a row of named sections, the way Learning Music's "Song structure" chapter teaches it. Each is a variation of the beat: same rows, different hits, some rows muted.
  • Intro / Verse / Chorus is the vocabulary people arrive with. Bars and clip edges are hidden inside a section.

What it takes

  • Roles over engines: a table in src/library mapping role → engine + default asset/preset, and role, key and tempo tags on the manifest (the builders in scripts/starter-library). trackCreation adds by role.
  • Scale-locked grid: a filter over src/editor/pianoRoll rows using the project key (CF-018). Mostly a view change.
  • Sections: either real structure (a Section entity grouping placements, a contract change) or a convention (sections are aligned 8-bar clips named the same on every track, which the commands can keep in step). The convention ships in weeks; the entity is Variant 3's cost.

Contracts touched

  • Library manifest and the Pack schema (tags). Editor views. Possibly the domain, if sections become an entity.
  • Instrument view survives as a "Tune" sheet for people who want the faders.

Cost

L

A path, not a pick

These compose

Variant 1 is a skin, and every other variant can wear it. Variant 2 is a layout, and Variants 4 and 5 both assume it (the beat and the song on one page, the tools in a drawer). Variant 4's two ideas, roles instead of engines and a grid that only shows the key, are independent of each other and of everything else. Variant 5 is a gate in front of whatever editor exists. Variant 3 is the one that does not compose: a pattern entity is a different song model, and it is the choice to make deliberately or not at all.

So the order that gets a beginner furthest soonest: 1 (one decision about the theme rules), then 2 (the layout and the coach strip, which reuses the lessons), then 4's roles and scale grid, then 5's stages on top. Sections (4 and 5's song view) come when the structure question is settled: a convention over aligned clips first, an entity only if the convention breaks.

The real brief

What it takes so nobody needs a tutorial video

A tutorial video exists to say what the screen does not. Each rule below is one thing the screen can say instead, with the variant that does it best and the place in the code it would live.

Open on the thing you came to do

The first screen is the beat, playing, with the squares you will click already in front of you. Today the project opens on an arrangement with one block on it.

Best in 3 and 5 · DEFAULT_EDITOR_VIEW, starterProject.ts

Every control has a word on it

Icons alone are a memory test. A label under the dock, "Play" on the play button, "Fill a pattern" instead of Generate. The hover tip is for the second visit, not the first.

All variants from 1 · ViewDock, EditorHeader, GeneratePanel

Name things by what they are for

Kick, not BD. Drums, not drum machine. Keys & bass, not sampler. The engine is an implementation detail until someone asks for the faders.

The empty space teaches

A dashed ghost where the next clip goes. One sentence under the grid. A "+ Pad" row in the grid, not a button in another view. The help is in the place the action happens.

1, 2 and 4 · EmptyView, the arrangement canvas, StepEditor

One next thing, always visible

Coproducer's lesson already knows the next bite and can do it for you. Put that sentence where the eye rests, not in a side panel you have to open.

2 and 5 · src/lessons, AssistantPanel → a coach strip

Hear every choice in context

A sound auditioned alone tells you nothing about whether it fits. Audition it in the beat, on the steps it will play, with the loop running.

2, 4 and 5 · audition.ts, CF-030's try-on-pad

Fewer screens, more tabs with words

Five views on number keys is a keyboard shortcut dressed as navigation. A drawer with Steps · Notes · Sound · Mix is the same five jobs with the structure on show.

2 onwards · editorViews.ts, EditorView.tsx

Show only what the person can use yet

Faders, sends, returns, stem export and the piano roll are right for week three. On day one they are noise. Unfold them when a step needs them, and remember where the person got to.

5 · a stage gate over the panes, src/memory

No wrong notes on the first day

A grid that shows only the key's notes turns a bassline from a theory exam into pattern-making. The key is already a project property.
