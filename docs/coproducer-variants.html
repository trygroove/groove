Groove · design study · onboarding, round 2

Coproducer in the studio

Round 1's variants 3, 4 and 5 got a beat playing fast by changing the tool. This round keeps one tool, the editor as it is or lightly reskinned (round 1's variants 1 and 2), and makes Coproducer the thing that teaches: it carries the lesson, recommends a video when a word needs one, and proposes the next change to your track so you hear it before you keep it. Five placements. A to D run the journey (first beat, build it out, finish and share) with the assistant in four places; E, added after a friend's suggestion, puts the conversation on the left and shows the same editor at three stages of a producer graduating out of it.

  1. Make tracks, not noise. Every screen is the real editor, and the journey ends with a track other people can hear.
  2. One tool. Nothing unfolds or hides by skill level. What changes is only what Coproducer says and where.
  3. Quick start. There is an order (the lesson chapters that exist: beat, bass, chords, arrange, mix and export) but no rails. Skip anything.
  4. Daily driver. Once you know the workflow, Coproducer is a button, a line, or a quiet column, and the keys do everything.

Every mock plays. Hear it previews a proposal in the beat as a striped row; Keep makes it real; Skip removes it. The mocks use round 1's Daylight skin so the assistant is easy to see; the same placements work on the monochrome theme.

Variant E · the conversation on the left, the music on the right

Left column, graduate right

A friend's suggestion, and the clearest graduation model so far. Coproducer's panel docks on the left, so the reading order is conversation first, music second. Week one you say what you want and watch it happen on the right, and every reply ends with how it was done in the editor. Week three you do most of it yourself and Coproducer comments. By day ninety the column is a rail and the ask box is a command palette. The tool never changes; the producer moves right across it.

Three stages of the same tool

Week one

You: say what you want, listen, undo. Coproducer: does it, flashes where, says how. The lesson: is the chips, in order.

The tool: the whole editor, on the right.
Week three

You: do most of it, ask "how" and "why". Coproducer: answers with the key and the gesture, notes what you did by hand, offers "Want me to?". The lesson: the chapters, behind "What's next?".

The tool: the same editor; you dragged the column narrower.
Day ninety

You: keys and the mouse; ⌘K when a sentence is faster. Coproducer: a rail, a palette, a master check before export. The lesson: over; the next track starts from this one.

The tool: the same editor, 40px narrower. Stems out when you outgrow it.

What it takes

  • A docked-left home for the panel: assistantPanelLayout.ts has floating, minimised, docked (right) and closed; this adds a side, and EditorView.css's --assistant-dock-space grows a left counterpart (the dock recentres over the views either way, as it does today).
  • "How I did it": every assistant change goes through the command layer, and each command has a summarize. This adds a table from command type to its UI home (view, key, control), and a folded line under each applied turn built from the entries it committed.
  • The flash: UI-004's control marks already outline a control a proposal touched (dashed while previewed, solid once landed). This widens it to a timed flash on a pad row and a clip.
  • The palette: a view.ask shortcut and a surface over useAskEditorLink with Do it / Show me / Explain, the three things a turn can already be (apply, propose, reply).

Contracts touched

  • Shortcut registry (⌘J, ⌘K). Panel layout contract (a side). Analytics: a how_opened event and the palette's.
  • Domain and commands untouched: the narration reads what exists.

Cost

M
  • Docked-left and the flash: S, and they ship first. "How I did it": M, and it is where the quality lives (evals on whether the line is right). The palette: M, after.

Recommendation

E is the frame; B, C and D fit inside it

E answers the question the other four were circling: not where to put the assistant, but which way a producer reads the screen over time. Conversation on the left is where you start; the music on the right is where you end up; and the bridge is Coproducer saying, every time it does something, how it did it in the editor's own words. The tool is identical at every stage, which is the one-tool principle exactly, and the end of the journey is stems for Ableton or FL, which is the make-tracks principle taken seriously.

The others survive as parts of it. B's rule, one proposal at a time, previewed in place with Hear and Keep on it, is how a week-three "Want me to?" should land. C's Next line is what the right side shows when the column is a rail and the producer still wants the order. D's feed is what the left column should look like once there is history in it: moves with outcomes, not a transcript. A is E with the column on the wrong side.

Ship order: docked-left and the flash (small, and the reading order changes immediately), then "How I did it" over the command summaries (the quality is there, and it needs evals), then the palette. Proposals previewing in place come alongside, because every variant leans on them.
