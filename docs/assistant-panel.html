Groove · AI-004 · assistant mockup · lessons (GRV-190)

An assistant that floats over the work, and can dock beside it

Step through the flow, or just use it: type in the composer, preview and apply the proposal, hear the beat with the play button, switch views, open the Lessons list and run a lesson from the plan at the top, and minimise, dock or close the panel. Replies are scripted and sounds are synthesized stand-ins.

Decisions this mockup takes, to check

  1. Three homes, one conversation. Floating (bottom-right, 384px wide, over the view), minimised (a 40px bar that keeps status), docked (a right column the view reflows around). Drag the top edge to set the floating height (260–720px) and the left edge to set the docked width (300–640px); arrow keys work on a focused edge, and a double-click resets it. The mode and both sizes are remembered on this device, not synced and not saved in the project. Closing hides it but keeps the conversation; the top-bar button and ⌘J bring it back where it was.
  2. Scope is visible and one click to change. The chip in the composer says what the assistant will read and may change: your selection when there is one, else the selected track, or the whole song. Each sent message is stamped with its scope.
  3. Nothing changes until Apply. A proposal lists every control it would change, old to new. Preview opens the view that shows the change and plays it there with dashed outlines; Cancel goes back to where you were. Apply commits one transaction and one undo step, then outlines what changed in solid white.
  4. A proposal can go out of date. If the song changes under it (your edit, or a change from another tab), the card says so, Apply is off, and Refresh asks for a new one.
  5. Explanations are one click deep. "Why this works" names the goal, the technique and the changed controls; each control is a link that points at it in the view. No lesson is mandatory.
  6. Pack and video cards are proposals too. Trying a sound previews it on the pad; Keep or Put back decide. A video plays in the card, can pop out larger over the view, and "Show me" points at the control it teaches.
  7. The lesson is a header, not a card. While a lesson runs, its plan sits at the top of the panel under the header, above the conversation: the lesson's name and where it stands, a strip with one column per chapter and one tick per bite (white when done, hatched when skipped, dim grey when the song already had it, outlined for the one under way), the chapter's bites as chips, and the step with Show me and Skip. It is one record per project, so the same plan comes back on a reload, another tab or another device, at the same step. It folds to its first two rows while you talk, and the title reads "Assistant · Lesson" until you leave. The conversation under it is Coproducer's words only; the plan is what you act on.
  8. Lessons are a list in the header, started with one press. The Lessons button shows every lesson in place of the conversation, the tour first, each with its progress as the same tick strip; pressing a row starts it, or continues the one under way, with no message sent and no model turn. The model never decides whether a lesson starts.
  9. Monochrome, square, shared parts. The panel uses the ramp, the product's buttons and chips, and brightness for state; the only hue is the track colour the proposal touches.
